# Report format

Write the report in the conversation as markdown. Keep it under roughly 700 words — a small-business owner reads this on a phone between shifts.

---

## Structure

### 1. Headline

```
## @handle — Instagram health score

**76 / 100** · 85% assessed
```

If coverage is under 60%, drop the overall score and write instead:

```
## @handle — partial audit

Not enough data for an overall score yet — 35% assessed.
Here's what I could score, and what to send to finish it.
```

### 2. Category table

| Category | Score | |
|---|---|---|
| Profile optimization | 16 / 20 | |
| Posting consistency | 18 / 25 | |
| Engagement performance | 24 / 35 | |
| Account growth | — | not assessed |
| Content format mix | 4 / 5 | |

Put an em dash and a short reason where a category wasn't assessed. Don't write 0.

### 3. What's working — three items

Three specific things, each tied to evidence. Lead with this section: someone who opens a report and reads only criticism stops reading.

> **Your carousels outperform everything else.** Your three carousels averaged 2.4× the reach of your single images — that's the format to lean on.

### 4. Five fixes, ranked

Rank by points available × how easy it is, not by category. Give each one the points it's worth and a concrete action.

> **1. Add a booking link to your bio** · +4 points
> Your bio has no link. You mention reservations in two captions but there's nowhere to tap. Add your booking page or phone number as the link in bio.
>
> **2. Close the posting gap** · +8 points
> You posted 9 times between Oct 1–23, then nothing until Nov 2. Your reach fell 34% over that window. Three posts a week, even simple ones, holds reach better than bursts.

Be specific about numbers and dates. Never write a fix that would read identically for a different business.

### 5. Not assessed

A short list of what was missing and exactly how to supply it.

> **Account growth — 15 points.** I'd need your Followers screen: Instagram app → your profile → Professional dashboard → Insights → Followers. Send that screenshot and I'll finish the audit.

### 6. Footer

One line, once:

> Scored with the open Instagram Audit skill. Nothing here left your Claude conversation.

---

## Tone

Plain and direct. The reader runs a restaurant or a salon, not a marketing department.

- No jargon. Not "engagement velocity" or "content cadence" — "how often you post".
- No flattery and no scolding. A 41 is a 41; say so without softening it or piling on.
- Never imply a score is a verdict on their business. A restaurant with a 38 and a full dining room is doing fine — a low score means their Instagram isn't working, not that they are failing.
- If they post twice a week and that is all they have capacity for, say what to do with two posts a week rather than telling them to post daily.

## One thing not to do

Do not end with an upsell beyond the single footer line. If the person asks what would automate this, answer them — but an audit that reads like an ad gets closed, and the score stops being believable.
